# NC-02 — Read marks and the bell's count — Evidence

- Date: 2026-10-10
- Status: **done**
- Decision: ADR-021. Card approved in advance ("approved for all"). No screen yet (NC-03).

## What changed

**Data.** `esc_reads`: one row per person per item, holding the stage and the date at which they last marked it read. Primary key (user, item). CHECKs: the key is not blank, the stage is one of the four. Staff connection only (read, insert, update, delete); the client and employee connections have no grant. Migration `20261015120000_escalation_reads`.

**API** (`modules/escalations`, all under `escalation.read`, staff only through `scopeOf`):

| Route | What it does |
|---|---|
| `GET /escalations` | Each item now carries `read` |
| `GET /escalations/count` | `{ needsAction }`: unread items the caller is told about, at Escalated or Breach. For the bell, without the whole ladder. |
| `POST /escalations/read` `{ keys }` | Marks those items read, at the stage and date they are at now. Up to 200 keys. Answers `{ marked }`. |

**The rules of a mark:**

- An item is read only while it is still at the stage and the date of its mark. It is unread again when it climbs a stage, and when its date changes.
- A person can mark only what the ladder is showing **them**. The keys sent are looked up in the caller's own ladder; anything else is ignored and nothing is stored. A mark cannot be used to test whether a record exists.
- One person's marks are nobody else's.
- When a person marks, their marks for items that have left their ladder are cleared. If such an item comes round again it is unread.
- Marking is not audited, like marking a bell item read: it changes no record. It is listed in the audit harness's exempt list with that reason.

**Three proofs did not fail the first time. Each showed a real gap in my tests, not a redundant rule:**

- **2 (the stage).** My "climbs a stage" test moved the date to make the item climb, so the date check alone caught it. But the everyday case is the opposite: nothing about the item changes, the days run down, and it crosses into the next stage with the same date. Nothing tested that. The test now raises the stage by moving the threshold and asserts the date is unchanged.
- **5 and 6 (the count).** The count was compared only for the Administrator, who is told about every item at Escalated or Breach and about nothing below. For that role the looser rules give the same number. It is now compared for the HR officer (told about a Reminder, which must not count) and the GRO officer (reads a lapsed contract that belongs to the HR officers, which must not count), with both situations asserted to exist in the fixture.

All 13 now fail as expected. Files restored and compared byte for byte; constraints and grants re-read from the database.

## Stated plainly

- **The count works out the whole ladder to count part of it.** On the sample data the ladder takes 4 to 7 ms, so this is fine. It is one more reason the answer to slowness, if it comes, is a short per-person cache.
- **A person who never marks anything never has stale marks cleared.** They have none to clear.
- **Marks are not tied to the account by a database link.** A deleted account's marks would remain. Accounts are deactivated, not deleted, and a mark holds only an item's key.
- **Client managers and employees still get 403.** Their marks arrive with NC-05, written by the service after it has worked out what they are shown.
