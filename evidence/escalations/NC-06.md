## Correction (2026-10-11, found during INS-03)

The figures above were one too high. The local database still held one request from an earlier walk-through ("Family details", raised by the sample employee on 7 October), past its due date. It was counted as a seeded item: one of the two request breaches, one of the client manager's two items and one of the employee's three. Taking that one row away, a clean seed gives HR 28 items (17 needing action), GRO 29 (still 14 needing action), the client manager 1 and the employee 2. These four are worked out by subtraction, not re-measured on that day's code; the INS-03 measurement on a clean seed agrees with them once its seven new items are added. No rule or test was affected; only the numbers quoted here. The landmine about checking for non-seed rows before quoting a seed figure was written in this very card, and this row slipped past it.

## Found while measuring

- **My first measurement after re-seeding was wrong, and it looked like a bug.** The GRO officer showed nothing assigned to them. The cause: the seed recreates the seed accounts with new ids, and I had reused a sign-in from before the re-seed, so the session carried an id that no longer owned anything. Signing in afresh gave the figures above. **After running Seed UAT, sign out and in again**, or "Assigned to me" will be empty until you do. Filed as SEED-02: the seed should end the sessions of the accounts it recreates.
- **My local database had 11 procedures the seed never made.** Earlier test runs had auto-opened them from the old expiry scan, and each one was quieting an expiry. That is why NC-01's evidence showed nine quiet items; on a clean seed there are five. I removed them locally. UAT is not affected: the scan is off there.
- I named a shell loop variable `path` again and lost every command in the loop. That is already in the guide's landmines; I re-ran with another name.

## Stated plainly

- **The client manager's sample ladder has no person on it**, only two requests past their service level, because the seed leaves the sample company's portal off (an earlier test relies on that default). Switch it on in Settings and a lapsed work permit appears. I checked that in NC-05.
- **The "something at every stage" smoke check depends on the calendar.** The seed's dates are relative to seed day, so it holds on the day of seeding and drifts afterwards. It is meant to be run as `seed-and-smoke`. Run as `smoke-only` days later, it could fail without anything being broken.
- **The mark-read smoke check leaves one read mark behind** on the HR sample account. It is harmless and goes when the accounts are next re-seeded.
- **Still not built, by your decision:** email when a stage is reached. Until that card, the old daily expiry scan (off by default) keeps its own thresholds.
