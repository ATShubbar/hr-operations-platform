## The measurement that motivated the card

```
nearest document expiry : 99 days out   → all four expiry tiles read 0
employees               : 4             → first page size is 25; pagination never engaged
job_title_ar            : empty on 3 of 4 → Arabic job-title search matched nothing
notif_notifications     : 1709 rows     → test-run pollution; the bell showed "64 unread"
```

Every date in `seed.ts` was a **hardcoded absolute** (`2027-03-15`, `2026-08-05`),
written when those were the near future. The fixture did not break — it **aged
out**, and would have aged further every day nobody looked. That is why the last
three cards each had to sabotage data by hand to see the states they built.

## The fix, in one line

Dates are now **relative to the moment the seed runs** (`daysFromNow(-4)`,
`daysFromNow(12)`), so the seed produces the same *shape* whenever it runs:
something overdue, something due this week, something at the edge of the 60-day
horizon.

## The mistake this card made, and how it surfaced

I gave the three new clients the obvious ids — `33333333-3333-4333-8333-…`,
`4444…`, `5555…`. **Three tests went red**, and correctly so: four e2e specs use
`33333333-3333-4333-8333-333333333333` as their sentinel for a client that does
**not exist** ("unknown client → 400/404"). Seeding it made the id real.

Fixed by moving the new clients to a `c1000000-…` range that nothing claims as
absent, and the seed now says so in a comment. This is exactly the risk the card
flagged, arriving from a direction I had not predicted — I expected trouble from
the expiry-scan tests, and those passed untouched.

**API suite: 336/336.**

## A flow demonstrating itself

After the suite ran, `gro_processes` held **17** rows against the seed's 9 — the
extra 8 all carry `source_document_id`. That is **GRO-05, the document-expiry →
GRO auto-spawn** (the fifth ADR-004 flow), firing on the new near-expiry
documents during a scan and opening one renewal process per document, exactly as
its idempotency guarantee says. Kept: it is realistic, and it is the flow working
on real data for the first time.
