## The measurement that shaped the whole card

The artwork is gold on navy. Against our surfaces:

```
#f7ce46 on the brand navy  #040a31   12.69:1   ✓
#f7ce46 on the sidebar     #fafafa    1.45:1   ✗
#f7ce46 on the page bg     #f7f7f5    1.41:1   ✗
```

The mark is legible on a dark ground and effectively **invisible** on a light one. That
is not a preference, it is why the sidebar could not simply take the file.

Owner chose the **navy chip**: a small plate carries the mark, so the brand's own colours
are preserved exactly and nothing about the logo is redrawn to suit our theme. The
alternative — generating a dark-mono wordmark — would have meant altering someone else's
logo on our own initiative.

`BrandMark` takes `plate={false}` for surfaces that are already dark, where a chip would
be a box around nothing.

## Where the name lives now

One key fed every site, so the rename is one line per locale.

| Site | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| browser tab | "HR Operations Platform" | **PEOPLE&GRO** |
| sidebar header | text | the mark, 132×14 in a 152×30 navy chip |
| mobile sheet | text | the mark, 120×12 |
| login card | text subtitle | the mark, 148px + the slogan |

`common.appName` is **PEOPLE&GRO in both locales**. A company name is not translated —
the same reasoning that keeps the wordmark Latin on the Arabic login. "HR Operations
Platform" survives as a descriptor, never as the name.

`auth.signInSubtitle` became the slogan: *Your Partner in HR Operations & Talent
Solutions* / *شريكك في عمليات الموارد البشرية وحلول المواهب*.

## The asset, and a bug caught in it

The wordmark is **cut from the artwork, not re-typeset**. An earlier attempt set it in
Inter at weight 200; Inter is a neo-grotesque and this is a geometric sans — circular O,
flat-crossbar G — so no weight would have matched. The glyphs are lifted at full
resolution and unpremultiplied against the flat navy sky, keeping their anti-aliased
edges instead of a keyed halo.

**The first extraction was wrong and it did not show on navy.** Median alpha across the
rectangle was **2/255** — sky sensor noise surviving as a faint dark box, invisible on the
original background and obvious the moment the mark sat on a light chip or a favicon tile:

```
before   median alpha 2   107,778 of 129,000 px with 0 < alpha < 25   → ghost box
after    median alpha 0   (anything under 6% treated as background, not edge)
```

Caught on the favicon, where the rectangle was unmistakable.

```
wordmark.webp   1020×105   17 KB
icon.png        512×512    the "&" glyph on a navy tile
apple-icon.png  180×180
```

The favicon is the **ampersand**, found by column-profiling the wordmark's alpha into ten
glyph runs and taking the seventh. It is the one letterform in the mark that is
distinctive on its own at 16px.

## Verified

| | |
|---|---|
| tab title | `PEOPLE&GRO`, both locales |
| sidebar (240px) | mark 132×14, chip `rgb(4, 10, 49)`, **7.73× pixels available** |
| mobile sheet (375px) | mark 120×12, inside the sheet, 0px page overflow |
| login card | mark 148px, RTL places it on the start edge |

**The mark is not announced twice.** In the sheet the dialog needs a text accessible name,
so the title stays as `sr-only` text and the image is `decorative` (`alt=""`) — measured,
the dialog's accessible name is `PEOPLE&GRO` and the image contributes nothing. Everywhere
else the image carries `alt="PEOPLE&GRO"`, because there the mark *is* the name.

The risk named in the card — that 14px of thin gold strokes would be too fine — did not
materialise; checked on screen rather than assumed, and there is 7.73× resolution behind
those 14 pixels.
