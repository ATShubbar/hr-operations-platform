## What the card found before any code

1. **Three controllers — requests, vacancies, GRO, eight call sites — chose the data path
   with `principalType === 'client_rep' && clientId ? client : staff`.** That is "anything
   that is not a well-formed client rep gets the CROSS-CLIENT staff path": an `employee`
   principal would have landed there the moment it held one of those permissions, and so
   would a client rep without a company. Fixed first.
2. **Auth cannot derive the employee's company** (that would read `emp_employees`, another
   module's table, from a foundation module). The session carries `employeeId` only; the
   company is resolved by self-service when needed (SS-03). Recorded as ADR-011 rev. 1.

### One deviation from the card, stated

The card said the employee role would have an **empty** permission list. Logout is
permission-gated (`session.end`), so an empty list meant an employee could sign in and
**not sign out** — contradicting the card's own DoD ("sign in, see who it is, sign out").
The role holds exactly `session.end`. It is not data access.

## ADR-011 rev. 1 (recorded in the ADR)

1. Company derivation: "at sign-in" → "when needed, by self-service" (layering, above).
2. **ADR-011's permission list is unsafe as written** — it grants the employee role
   `employee.read`/`salary.read`/`govdata.read`/`document.read`, which are the permissions
   the STAFF list endpoints check, and those endpoints don't look at the principal. Granted
   literally, every employee account could read every client's employees. Resolution is
   SS-03's card (a dedicated self-service permission, the `portal.read` pattern, and/or a
   principal fence on staff-only endpoints). Found by reading the controllers for this card.
