### Deviation from the card, stated

The card gated everything under `/me` on `self-service.read`. I first wired the POST that
way, then changed it: a WRITE behind a `.read` permission breaks the architecture's
`resource.action` convention. `POST /me/requests` requires **`self-service.create`** —
employee-only, never a staff resource name, so the principal fence still holds.

## Found by the first run

Every employee-raised request returned **500: `permission denied for sequence
aud_entries_id_seq`**. `aud_entries.id` is a sequence, and `INSERT` on the table does not
include `USAGE` on its sequence — Postgres refuses at `nextval` before RLS is evaluated.
AUDIT-02 hit the identical finding for `app_client` (`20260721160826_audit_client_seq_grant`).
Fixed in its own migration rather than editing an applied one.

| **DB, raw `app_employee` scoped to me** | own well-formed insert accepted; refused by RLS: raised as a colleague · another company · closed · high priority · due date · pre-assigned; UPDATE + DELETE of my own → permission denied; audit insert for another company → RLS; reading the audit trail → permission denied |
| gates | company not opted in → 403 (both routes); terminated → 403; staff + client rep → 403 (both); unauthenticated → 401 (both) |

**Proven to bite:** `ALTER POLICY employee_raise … WITH CHECK (true)` → **5 of the 6** forged
inserts succeeded (another company, closed, high priority, due date, pre-assigned) — as they
should with the rule gone. The sixth, "raised as a colleague", was **still refused**: Prisma's
insert uses `RETURNING`, and Postgres also checks the SELECT policy against the returned row —
`employee_own_read` hides a row raised as someone else. Two layers for that case. Policy
restored and compared **byte-identical** to the original (`pg_get_expr`) → 24/24.
